# UFO Catcher

UFO Catcher is a reusable paid mini-game type first used by the Celadon Game
Corner claw machine. Celadon's standard and High Stakes cabinets are launched
from the corresponding objects in the `Game Plaza` Grid Adventure.

## Player flow

Each attempt creates a five-prize cabinet. The claw starts at the front-left
corner. The player holds the first control to move it right, releases to lock
that axis, then holds and releases the second control to send it away from the
player toward the back of the cabinet and drop.

The hold control accepts only the primary pointer button, captures that pointer
until release, and suppresses selection, browser callouts, and context menus.
Keyboard Space and Enter holds remain supported. The idle paid-play control is
text-only; movement arrows appear only while positioning the claw.

The single control button gives the current movement instruction and depresses
during a hold; losing focus or pointer capture ends that hold. The cabinet keeps
its original ungridded floor and crosshair, without a separate page title or
step panel. The last completed catch, slip, or miss stays visible above the play
button with guidance for the next attempt. This feedback does not
change travel speed, capture footprints, grip odds, or server settlement.

The cabinet selects five of seven authored anchor regions without replacement,
then applies bounded horizontal and depth jitter. This gives every paid reveal a
different arrangement without allowing prizes to overlap the claw's playable
bounds. Mobile and desktop layouts only scale the presentation. The overhead
carriage, cable, claw head, and arms animate independently: the carriage follows
both positioning moves, the cable lowers to the locked floor coordinate, the
arms close around the resolved prize, and the claw then lifts before returning
to the prize chute or showing a failed grip.

The machine uses the standard PokeOri clay-and-parchment palette rather than
night-activity chrome. Its collection chute is communicated by the recessed
ramp, hinged flap, and delivery motion without a visible text or present label.
Before an attempt, a solid clay shutter hides the cabinet contents and slides
up after the stake is accepted. Rotom appears on both the machine marquee and
the Explore game entry, while the Explore list gives UFO Catcher its own
joystick game-type icon.

UFO Catcher never returns Fun Tokens. The server makes five weighted draws
without replacement when the attempt starts, so the revealed prizes are always
distinct. Common medicine, gems, and materials fill most cabinets, with rarer
X items, special Poké Balls, evolution items, valuables, EV training prizes,
and profile unlocks appearing through the same visible pool. A profile icon or
title is removed from a trainer's eligible pool after they unlock it.

Capture footprints deliberately tighten with prize rarity: common prizes use a
36-unit radius, uncommon prizes 32, rare prizes 27, and ultra-rare prizes 22.
At the authored carriage speeds, a common prize permits roughly
180ms of horizontal error when the depth is exact; the two-axis circular check
makes simultaneous errors less forgiving.

## Runtime integrity

Starting an attempt deducts the stake and creates a five-minute Redis record at
`ufo-catcher:${user.id}`. It stores the public layout and a private grip roll.
Starting again while that record is active restores the paid attempt without
charging again.

The client submits only the two hold durations. The server converts those
durations into authored coordinates, selects the closest overlapping prize, and
resolves its grip chance. Client-reported positions and prize identities are not
trusted. Weighted prize selection, anchor selection, position jitter, and the
private grip roll are all generated by the server.

Settlement uses the standard action lock, rate-limit, idempotency, reward, and
game-result patterns. A caught prize records a win; a miss or slip records a
loss. Explicitly leaving during a paid unresolved attempt forfeits the stake and
records a loss.

## Celadon balance

The standard Game Plaza UFO Catcher costs 30 Fun Tokens. Its High Stakes
counterpart costs 300; Match 3 and Prize Wheel keep their own existing stakes.
The standard pool has EV feathers and Up-Grade, but no EV vitamins or Dubious
Disc. The High Stakes pool removes the feathers and Up-Grade, adds the six EV
vitamins and Dubious Disc, includes all eleven evolution stones, and adds a
Super Potion. Tier-one material quantities rise from three to fifteen, and the
Team Rocket Binder's owned-item replacement rises from one to five booster
packs.

In the High Stakes pool, Up-Grade and EV feathers are replaced by Dubious Disc,
the six EV vitamins, all eleven evolution stones, and Super Potion. Each added
evolution stone and Dubious Disc has weight 0.5 (rare); each vitamin has weight
0.5 (ultra rare); Super Potion has weight 8 (common). Material prize quantities
are five times the standard amount, and the binder replacement awards five
booster packs.

Weights are relative and recalculate after each selected entry is removed. The
grip profiles are 45–95% for common prizes, 30–85% for uncommon, 15–70% for
rare, and 0–55% for ultra rare from the edge to the exact centre.

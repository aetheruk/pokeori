# Battle System

Turn-based Pokemon battle system with advanced mechanics.

## Locations
- Routes: `/game/battles/*`
- Components: `src/components/game/battles/`
- Engine: `src/utilities/battle/engine/`
- Data: `src/data/battles/`, `src/data/powers.ts`

## Mechanics
The six command buttons occupy only their padded three-row grid; the battle log takes the remaining lower space without a Powers counter. Choosing Stance, Moves, Items, Powers, Switch, or Flee replaces that combined lower region with a shared scrollable section, fixed Back/title header, and remaining-use chip where applicable. The three-win Powers counter appears only when Powers is open. Forced lead/replacement selection cannot be dismissed. Move notes, revive targeting, and flee confirmation stay inside the section. The Stance section draws a different type-specific canvas scene for every type: embers, waves, vines, lightning, frost, and other physical motifs. The animation pauses when hidden and renders statically under reduced motion.

Every Pokémon Power requires three successful stance-matchup wins from a shared, spendable team pool in PvE and a separate pool per participant in PvP. Each winning doubles lane can contribute one; a failed, missed, or blocked action cannot earn a win. Activation spends three wins in addition to existing assignment, research, Trainer level, key-item, use-limit, and exclusivity checks. Turn-count and Dimensional Shift's separate win/loss/draw charges no longer gate activation. The pool is shown beside the battle log and in the Powers header.

Arming a Z-Move is immediate and does not spend a turn. It survives other actions and failed/missed attacks until the next successful basic stance attack, or until switching/fainting/battle end. That attack is named `{Type} {Strike|Tackle|Gambit} Z` and has base power 250 before modifiers; an active Dynamax stance is named `MAX {Type} {Attack}` with base power 100. Z takes precedence for one attack if both states overlap. Successful attackers on both sides advance even when they lose the stance comparison; only the stance winner gets a bright gold halo and sprite glow during the exchange. Failed or blocked actors remain still. The battle log marks stance wins green, draws blue, and losses red.

### Battle Types
- **PvE**: Battle against gym leaders, wild Pokemon, NPCs
- **PvP**: Battle against other players (via `/game/battles/pvp/[id]`)
- Double-battle faint replacements use the shared inline TeamSwapper cards rather than a doubles-only list. They open in the lower command section for the next empty lane and advance after the server accepts a replacement. Sketch uses the chosen opposing lane as its capture target in both PvE and PvP and commits through the same post-win Sketchbook flow as singles.
- Doubles preserve the player's actor-selection order and resolve two ordered exchanges per turn: the first selected allied action pairs with the first opposing action, then the second pair resolves. Each pair uses the singles simultaneous-resolution rules, so both actors alive at the start of the pair can complete their action, while a Pokémon already fainted before its pair is skipped.
- Doubles history and presentation preserve that phase boundary: each exchange is shown as `Turn N - A` or `Turn N - B` with its own stance result and actions. Both successfully attacking actors advance; the victorious one also pulses.
- Doubles combat actions include their resolved target in the shared battle log. Special moves read as `Pokemon used Move on Target`, while generic stance attacks read as `Pokemon attacks Target with [stance]`; spread moves produce one target-specific action line per affected Pokémon.
- In a doubles exchange, the stance multiplier belongs only to the opposing Pokémon acting in that exchange. Spread damage to additional opponents or to the user's own team is neutral for stance purposes, while type, move, ability, and partner modifiers still apply normally.
- Authored move types are authoritative in doubles damage and battle-log presentation. The queued type selector is used for basic stance attacks only; special moves resolve their forced, dynamic, or Hidden Power type from move data.
- **Double battles**: Authored `format: 'double'` battles field two active Pokémon per side in PvE or PvP. Each healthy, eligible Pokémon takes one action in the selected actor order; two ordered lane exchanges resolve switches, trainer items/powers, priority moves, and attacks. Actions remain attached to their originating Pokémon across Ally Switch and voluntary switches. Players tap a living target sprite first, then choose a stance or move; a red arrow marks the target and an ochre arrow marks the actor. The first choice advances to the partner and the last submits the turn automatically. The same six-command/shared-section layout serves singles and doubles, including inline revive targeting and per-lane forced replacements. Four-lane presentation queues HP, logs, fainting, and switches; outgoing sprites remain until their switch animation finishes. Friendly and ranked doubles use separate PvP queues, and Kanto/Test has a reward-free repeatable trial. Doubles-only TMs are unusable in singles. Partner abilities, weather, terrain, statuses, held items, KO credit, and friendship are handled across active lanes. Existing singles states without `format` continue unchanged.
- Doubles expose Tera, Mega, Dynamax, immediately armed Z-Moves, Victory, Weather, Shout, Circadian, and Dimensional Shift in PvE and PvP. A global Time or Chaos shift applies to all four active Pokémon; targeted Weather and Circadian effects use the selected lane. Existing charged, recharge, and continuous move locks remain single-active/PvE mechanics and need a per-Pokémon doubles lock model before they can be offered in doubles.
- Ally-only doubles moves are withheld from the move drawer when the partner is fainted or Commander-inactive, since there is no active sprite to target.
- Doubles ground shadows use scaled singles-style foot offsets: each player's shadow sits lower than the opposing team's shadow, preserving the foreground/background perspective without a visible gap beneath the smaller sprites.
- **Spectated AI vs AI**: Battle Bets runs two Advanced AI teams through PvP's side-neutral shared turn resolver with normal battle persistence disabled. Placing a wager orients the backed team as the player side, the session persists the current battle state, and the standard client battle manager animates each resolved turn.
- Wild battle enemy entries can define `requirements`; the server filters locked enemies before rolling the wild opponent.

All temporary stat-stage changes reset when a Pokemon faints, including when it
is revived in place or by a party-revival move. Intentional stat-passing moves
can still carry stages from a surviving Pokemon to its replacement.

### Pokemon Setup
- PvE battle configs can set `playerTeamInitialStatus` to apply a forced main status to every loaded player-team member before turn one. This bypasses normal type and ability immunity because it represents an authored environmental condition; normal held-item status responses still resolve. Koga’s Gym uses this to poison the full team at the start of each trial battle.
- Kid Mode gives the player's Pokemon a battle-instance-only 20% boost to HP, Attack, Defense, Special Attack, Special Defense, and Speed in PvE. The multiplier is applied once after normal stat modifiers, survives switches and form transformations, and is never persisted to the owned Pokemon. Enemy teams, PvP, and Battle Bets simulations do not receive it.
- Owned Pokemon use assigned battle moves rather than every available move. Researcher level unlocks 1, 2, 3, and 4 usable move slots at levels 10, 20, 45, and 60. The species also needs Pokemon Research level 1 to assign or use those moves. TM/HM moves are gated by ownership and authored `formId` lists, not Pokemon type arrays; missing or empty `formId` lists mean no Pokemon can learn that move until learners are authored. Player assignments do not require the move's authored level, while enemy AI and automatic player fallback loadouts retain level-aware selection.
- Player-facing move details are generated from structured move data through one shared presentation model. MoveDex/Sketchbook, inventory, Pokédex compatibility notes, Pokémon loadouts, and battle use the same base-power, accuracy, target, effect, timing, condition, rule, and risk vocabulary. Pokémon details shows only its unlocked loadout slots; Manage moves opens a full-screen library with search plus type, stance, effect, source, and sort filters, compact assignment rows, and the complete field note behind a visible book action. Auto-pick stages a balanced selection through the same neutral loadout scorer used for automatic battle fallbacks, but the player must review and save it. In battle, a quick tap on a move uses it; holding, right clicking, or pressing I while its button is focused opens the complete field note without committing the turn. Battle does not display an offensive-stat value as though it were projected damage.
- Move descriptions are presentation copy only and do not determine runtime categories. Healing is identified from explicit healing, drain, status-cure healing, weather healing, or target-stat healing fields. `selfDamage.trigger` distinguishes recoil after a hit, crash damage after a miss, and HP costs on use; these semantics are shared by PvE, PvP, ability handling, and AI scoring.
- Sketch checks for a new eligible move on the opposing active Pokémon before its Research Level-scaled capture roll: 25% at Level 1, 30% at Level 2, 35% at Level 3, 40% at Level 4, and 50% at Level 5. A target with no TM-backed move reports that directly; a target whose eligible moves are all already in the player's Sketchbook reports that there is no new move. Only a non-empty new-move pool can produce a capture success or random capture failure, consistently in PvE and PvP.
- Owned Pokemon persist their own `battleKOs` counter. Battle resolution records KO credits when a Pokemon's recorded damage knocks out an opposing Pokemon, then writes those credits back to the Pokemon document when the battle ends or is surrendered.
- Regular PvE wild/trainer enemies roll missing IVs and EVs when battle starts. Wild Pokemon roll missing IVs once, use 0 EVs through level 40, and roll missing EVs from 1-100 above level 40. Trainer Pokemon roll missing IVs once below level 30, twice from level 30, and three times from level 50, keeping the best roll per IV; missing EVs roll from 1-70 below level 20, 1-150 from level 20, 50-252 from level 50, and 100-252 from level 70. Manual authored IV/EV values remain fixed.
- Assigned moves can author `charged` turn counts, `recharge` turn counts, or `continuous` min/max turn ranges. Charged moves spend generic charge turns before release without revealing the move name or stance. Recharge moves deal damage first, then lock the UI to a Recharge button for the authored number of turns; pressing it skips the player's attack while the enemy acts. Continuous moves lock the player into the same move button until the rolled duration ends or the move misses, fails, or is interrupted; while locked, normal stance attacks, items, switches, and other moves are blocked.
- Move configs can mark moves as `trainerOnly`, `aiOnly`, or `manualOnly` for bespoke cases. Broad TM libraries should remain normal move configs and use explicit `formId` authoring for learnability; missing or empty `formId` lists mean no Pokemon can learn that move, no generated TM is exposed, and no MoveDex assignment entry appears. When a battle Pokemon has `aiMoves`, that list is its complete special-move pool.
- Held item assignment unlocks at Trainer level 15 and Pokemon Research level 1 for that species. Type boost held items exist in three crafted tiers for all 18 Pokemon types: diminished versions give +10% matching-type damage with a 3% attack break chance, normal versions give +10% and never break, and Unstable versions give +15% with a 15% attack break chance; break chances only roll when the item actually boosts that attack's resolved type. Training Bands lower their matching battle stat by 15% while held, then silently grant 1 matching EV up to the 255 stat cap on battle wins with a 2% held-item consumption roll. Signature held items support species-gated stance damage, critical-hit stages, and stat multipliers: Light Ball doubles Pikachu's Power/Tech damage, Leek grants Farfetch'd and Sirfetch'd two critical-hit stages, Thick Club doubles Cubone and Marowak's Attack, and Ditto's Quick Powder and Metal Powder double Speed or Defense until it transforms. Lucky Egg gives its holder 1.5× battle experience and retains its 20% chance to double wild-battle candy quantities, while Lucky Punch doubles Chansey's critical-hit chance. Plate and Memory held items drive Multitype and RKS System active battle types, and battle-local item transfer, theft, swapping, removal, restoration, or consumption refreshes those types immediately. Eligible held items can trigger automatically in PvE and PvP. Consumed held items are unavailable for the rest of the active battle, then auto re-equip after battle only if another copy can be consumed from inventory. Move-caused held item transfer, theft, swapping, throwing, removal, and berry destruction are battle-only item state changes and do not consume inventory or permanently alter the Pokemon's assigned held item. Charge-based held items persist progress on `pokemon.itemCharge`; Inferior elemental stones gain 5% when hit by their matching attack type, reset when removed, and at 100% remove themselves to grant the matching evolution stone. Inferior Light/Dark Stones gain 2% from any damaging attack only during day/night windows; Light completes into Dawn Stone from 06:00-06:59 or Sun Stone otherwise, while Dark completes into Dusk Stone from 18:00-18:59 or Moon Stone otherwise.
- Pokemon Powers require the active Pokemon to have Pokemon Research level 3 for its species, in addition to the selected power, Trainer/key item requirements, and three unspent stance wins. Z-Moves use the Z-Ring and arm immediately; the next successful basic stance attack has base power 250, with the charge retained through other actions and misses.
- Battles inherit the weather snapshot resolved when the encounter starts. Weather modifies damage in PvE, PvP, special moves, and battle powers: sun/rain adjust Fire and Water damage, extreme sun blocks Water, heavy rain blocks Fire, sandstorm boosts Rock Sp. Def and damages non-Rock/Ground/Steel active Pokemon, hail damages non-Ice active Pokemon, snow boosts Ice Defense, snowstorm combines snow's Ice Defense boost with hail-style chip damage, fog lowers non-perfect move accuracy, strong winds neutralize Flying weakness from Electric/Ice/Rock attacks, shadowy aura damages non-Shadow active Pokemon, and sunlight prevents freeze.
- Weather-setting TMs include Sunny Day, Rain Dance, Sandstorm, Hail, and Snowscape. They are normal assigned moves with canonical machine compatibility and overwrite the battle-local weather when used. Castform research awards all five TMs at Research Levels 1 through 5, but Castform is intentionally not a Snowscape learner.
- Weather Power reads the current battle weather and performs a named weather technique: clear weather heals and grants Regeneration; sunlight, rain, thunderstorms, sandstorms, snow, fog, strong winds, and shadowy aura produce matching typed attacks or restorative/status effects. It works in PvE and PvP and does not consume Weather Cores.
- Basic Power, Speed, and Tech stance attacks have 100% base accuracy. Authored moves retain their configured base accuracy. The attacker's accuracy stage minus the defender's evasion stage forms one net stage clamped from -6 to +6, and the resulting modifier is rolled once for the whole move in both PVE and PVP. A successful hit then rolls only authored secondary-effect chances; it does not repeat the move accuracy check. Always-hit and accuracy-bypass effects still resolve at 100%. A simultaneously committed attack retains these modifiers even if earlier sequential processing has already reduced its user to 0 HP.
- Canonical main-game abilities are generated for all Pokemon forms. Battle runtime support covers active-Pokemon type immunities, type absorbs with healing/stat boosts, status immunities, Pokemon-type-gated status and stat-drop protections, mental effect protections, stat-stage protections/triggers/bypass, main-status reflection, main-status counter steps, priority-style move contests, terrain/weather setters and sub-rules, end-turn status cures, active battle type changes, held-item hooks, battle-win reward hooks, battle stat multipliers, final damage multipliers, ability type-effectiveness overrides, active ability copying, move and held-item scouting, field-scope move blocking, non-damaging status-move blocking/bounce logs, added-effect suppression/chance/damage modifiers on damaging moves, ability-granted same-turn interruption chance, post-damage attacker status riders, per-hit multi-hit damage application, extra-hit damage events, reactive chip and KO damage, full-HP survival, identity masking, and suppression, Wonder Guard, Disguise/Ice Face shields, and form-change abilities including entry and Tera-activation field hooks. Doubles redirection, true contact-only triggers, broad ability copying/masking, and move-flag-specific boosts remain unsupported or approximated.
- Moves can apply secondary statuses separate from the normal one-status slot. Secondary statuses can attach to a Pokemon, a side, both sides, or the whole field; they can trigger after normal end-turn damage/weather or when a Pokemon switches in. Reapplying the same secondary status ID to the same Pokemon, side, or field refreshes/replaces the existing instance instead of stacking duplicates. Pokemon-attached secondary statuses, such as Yawn-style delayed effects, are cleared whenever that Pokemon switches out; side and field statuses persist unless a move authors a side-scoped `secondaryStatusCure`. Supported effects include damage, absorb damage, healing, regular status application, status immunity, wake-status effects, stat stages with optional Pokemon-type filters, outgoing damage modifiers keyed by stance/type, damage reduction keyed by incoming stance/type, type-immunity bypass, infatuation, switch prevention, and move blocking. Foresight and Odor Sleuth use immunity bypass only; their canonical evasion-reset helper behavior is not separately modeled beyond standard accuracy/evasion stage logic. These effects log text only and do not add battle UI visuals.
- Moves can require stance wins with `failOnStance: 'tie'`; `preventCounterOnStanceWin` lets those stance-win moves also cancel the opponent's same-turn attack, as Sucker Punch does.
- Move configs also support reusable runtime effects for called moves, terrain setting, explicit multi-hit metadata, dynamic move typing, exact flat/current-HP damage rules, always-hit accuracy bypass for moves such as Kowtow Cleave, one-use next-damage and next-accuracy modifiers, conditional damage modifiers, post-damage status cures, post-damage stat-stage effects such as target-KO boosts, weather-aware healing, stat-stage copy/swap effects and type-filtered boosts, party/status cures, target status cures with optional user healing for moves such as Purify, secondary-status cures, party revival, forced or pending switches, move locks, target-status battle conditions, first-active-turn gates, held-item transfer/removal/theft/swapping/recycling/berry consumption, and battle item-use availability changes. Secondary statuses can also grant passive accuracy bypass for affected Pokemon while active. Secondary move-block statuses can block zero-damage moves, specific move IDs, repeated last-used moves, healing moves, or matching attack types with optional block damage. Stockpile-style item-use changes adjust current available battle uses only; they never create or consume inventory items or raise the battle's max item capacity.
- Player battle item use checks item skill requirements before consumption.
- Pokemon Research level 4 gives player-owned Pokemon a 2% chance to survive a KO blow at 1 HP if they are not already at 1 HP.

### Type Chart
Located in `src/utilities/battle/engine/type-chart.ts` - standard Pokemon type effectiveness.

Damage calculation uses a shared 50 BP baseline for normal stance attacks and 1x battle moves before stance, stats, type, weather, held-item, and other modifiers. It also includes reverse-STAB as a final multiplier: if the resolved attack type does not match any of the attacker’s effective battle types (including tera override), damage is reduced by 15%.

### Wild Battle Rewards
- Participating owned Pokemon gain battle experience from a normal PvE win using the Generation V level-scaled formula: each opposing Pokemon's base yield and level are adjusted by the gap between its level and the receiving Pokemon's persisted level. Trainer battles add the 1.5× trainer bonus, Lucky Egg adds 1.5×, healthy non-participants receive the default Exp. Share half, and a future point-power multiplier is supported. A temporary battle sync or level cap does not change the award; a level 5 wild Rattata therefore gives only a small award to a level 41 Pokemon. PvP never grants Pokemon experience. The Pokemon's official species growth curve determines its level, and the persistent badge cap starts at level 20, then rises by 5 for every Kanto or Johto badge to level 100 at all 16 badges. Existing Pokemon above the current cap are preserved and are not downgraded. The result screen presents these gains in an animated EXP section above the other rewards, with each recipient's sprite, progress bar, gain, and level-up state.
- Wild battles roll one Candy using the highest enemy level to choose the Candy tier, with a flat 8% chance. The old guaranteed trainer Candy and badge-support Candy rolls have been removed; the remaining authored story, task, expedition, shop, release, and crafting rewards use their current content-specific quantities and limits.
- Wild battles also roll Candy Dust at 30%. The quantity scales with the highest enemy level from 1 at levels 1-20, 1-2 at 21-40, 1-3 at 41-60, 2-3 at 61-80, and 3 at 81-100. Candy Dust is a misc material and is not multiplied by Lucky Egg.
- Releasing a Pokemon has a 10% chance to return one Candy from its level tier. The release still returns held items and Broken Ball casings as before, with researched Pokemon also producing their material rolls.
- Battle wins give each involved player Pokemon 1 Pokemon Research XP. Wild battle Pokemon gem rewards grant one guaranteed base-type gem per qualifying reward pass. Pokemon Research no longer adds extra candy rolls to wild battle wins.
- Owned Pokemon lose 1 friendship, clamped at 0, each time they faint in PVE or PVP battles. NPC trainer and wild enemy Pokemon do not persist friendship changes.
- Authored early Kanto trainer battles use original Red/Blue-style prize money: trainer class base payout times the level of the trainer's last Pokemon.
- Losing or surrendering a normal trainer battle deducts up to half of that battle's configured PokeDollar win reward and shows the loss on the defeat result screen. Wild battles, PVP, chronicles, reward-disabled battles, and battle configs with `disableLossPayout` do not apply this payout loss.
- VS Seeker battles appear at the top of Explore when the `vs-seeker` key item is ready after its 30-minute cooldown. The Explore modal uses the standard battle stat chips for generated enemy level and team size, and the standard reward carousel for the reward bundle. They use 3 non-legendary, non-mythical Pokemon sampled from the player's seen Pokedex forms, cap the player team at the generated trainer level, use the `trainer` enemy AI profile until the player owns all 8 Kanto badges, then use `advanced`, and pay 1000 PokeDollars plus 1 League Ticket. Their opposing Pokemon still award normal PvE experience, but they no longer include the old guaranteed Candy bundle. VS Seeker defeats and surrenders do not deduct PokeDollars.
- The Legendary Bird special battles for Moltres, Zapdos, and Articuno require Trainer level 50 before they can appear.
- Kid Mode players cannot enter friendly or ranked PvP. PvP entries are requirement-gated out of Explore, and lobby, queue, status, and shared-battle initialization paths enforce the restriction server-side.
- The battle Moves section uses the same full-width bar design as the Stance section. Each move follows the fill color of its type sprite across all stances; Fire is slightly darkened and Normal has a light silver treatment. A darkened trailing edge keeps every attack name white and readable, including on light types. Each bar shows a white stance icon on an angled darker wedge and keeps only the move name visible. Base power, accuracy, target, conditions, costs, and other details remain in the generated field note. Singles and doubles use the same move buttons and detail interaction.
- Normal stance controls use three shades of the selected attack type based on the same type sprites. Each button has an angled darker wedge, a plain white stance icon, and its attack name. Pressing a stance immediately submits the existing action and plays a brief arrow dash, impact, or orbit inside that button; reduced-motion clients keep the icons static. Pending stance requests show an inline indicator without a covering overlay. The compact type row retains the actual type images, and Items, Moves, and Powers retain their existing item/TM/power sprites beside labels and use counts. Singles and doubles share the stance and action-trigger presentation, with no extra stance heading.
- The battle action panel keeps its section name in the top bar, without repeating Moves, Items, or Switch inside the content. Lead selection and later switches use the full two-column panel width. Moves and Powers display the active Pokemon's primary-type atmosphere behind the controls; Items shows the artisan workshop beneath a stronger paper overlay so item text stays dominant. Those backgrounds stay fixed while the action lists scroll, and Flee centers its confirmation vertically in the panel. The VS card reveal shows the current battle scene behind a dark overlay instead of a plain blackout.
- Battle HUD status chips use status-specific colors with paired high-contrast text and canonical display names for damaging, control, restorative, veil, and shield effects.
- Each battle-log action group with an actual stance exchange centres a bold, white-labelled result above the action: `STANCE WIN` uses vivid green, `STANCE DRAW` uses vivid blue, and `STANCE LOSS` uses vivid red. Setup, send-out, item-only, and passive-effect entries do not display a synthetic stance result.
- Battle history reads as one chronological top-to-bottom timeline: older turns appear above newer turns, entries within a turn retain their action order, and live updates keep the viewport at the newest entry. The compact view shows the latest four turns; its older-history control sits above the timeline because expanded turns are inserted there.
- Battle Pokemon selection rows show HP once as an exact current/max value beneath the visual health bar; they omit the redundant percentage and heart icon.

## State Management
- `useBattleManager.ts`: Main battle state manager
- New battle results carry one bounded server-authored semantic presentation sequence. The visual manager plays attacks, exact HP targets, residual damage/healing, simultaneous ties, incremental log lines, faints, and switch/replacement events in that order, then applies the final authoritative state. Switch events preserve the incoming Pokemon's entry HP instead of installing its final end-of-turn HP early, so a later hit splat and health-bar drop occur together. Replacement entry damage/healing markers resolve only after that Pokemon is visibly switched in. Presentation reconstruction follows authored switch and faint lines even when a side ends the turn on the same active index it started with; a voluntary switch whose incoming Pokemon is KO'd therefore plays the switch-out, incoming hit/KO, faint, and replacement return rather than collapsing to the unchanged final index. Player-selected replacements submitted after a faint author an explicit switch-in event even though the outgoing Pokemon was already fainted in that action's baseline; final-state synchronization also clears stale faint animation state whenever a healthy active Pokemon is present. A terminally fainted active Pokemon remains hidden until the result screen takes over instead of flashing back into view after its faint animation. Older Redis battle states without this field continue through the legacy diff event generator.
- Battle controls submit through the validated same-origin `/api/game/battles/action` JSON endpoint rather than waiting for a Next.js Server Action route-render response. The endpoint dispatches stance, authored move, item, switch, power, and surrender actions through the existing server-side authentication, validation, idempotency, and locking paths, while the client keeps the established attack, impact, HP, status, KO, and replacement animation timings.
- PVE and PVP battle creation snapshots the server-validated move IDs available to each player Pokemon after TM ownership, form, explicit assignment, Researcher slots, battle-cap-aware fallback selection, opponent team, and AI profile are applied. Turn actions validate against that snapshot and retain a live inventory fallback for older serialized battles, avoiding an inventory collection query on every authored move. Basic stance attacks never require inventory validation.
- Successful damaging attacks include optional `playerAttackType` / `enemyAttackType` battle-log metadata. The visual queue uses that resolved type at one shared impact frame: both committed attacks apply their hit motion, splats, and HP targets simultaneously, including Shadow scream self-damage; same-frame attack and self-damage amounts targeting one Pokemon are summed into one readable splat. A stance tie moves both attacking sprites, while a stance win moves only the winning side. Final authoritative HP reconciliation is folded into the last authored HP impact where possible so bars do not visibly correct upward after an attack without a healing event. Healing renders as moss `+HP` feedback. Reduced-motion clients preserve event ordering without waiting through animation delays. Battle stat-stage messages use full Pokémon-standard labels such as `Special Attack` and `Special Defense`. The battle log keeps the existing server message contract but progressively reveals newline-separated messages and inline icon tokens as their effects resolve, then reconciles to the authoritative turn entry. Battle controls use taller stance buttons, sprite-icon item/power triggers with inline item/move use counts, and an icon-only switch trigger. The player's Pokemon HUD sits at the bottom of the sprite stage.
- Turn resolution uses the same priority contract in PVE and PVP: trainer items and voluntary switches resolve before attacks; both eligible primary actions are committed before HP changes; primary move healing is settled against the full incoming-damage pool; primary KOs are checked before queued move pivots; surviving pivots resolve before held-item healing; residual damage resolves before survivor-only status, terrain, ability, and rarity healing; ordinary faint replacements enter only after the end phases. A pivot user that faints cannot switch, but a surviving Volt Switch/U-turn-style user still pivots after knocking out its target. Held HP berries cannot interrupt the two committed attacks.
- If both PVP teams are exhausted by the same primary/residual settlement, the stance result decides a win or loss and a tied stance (`STANCE DRAW` in the log) produces a neutral battle draw with no PVP reward or win/loss activity record. PVE uses a stance win as the player victory and treats a stance loss or draw as defeat. Battle Bets converts a shared-engine draw through its existing judge/fallback winner so wagers always settle.
- Battle presentation never emits a visible anonymous HP correction. Authored damage/healing tokens—including tokens on stance-action lines—drive effects, while any legacy log-parser mismatch is diagnosed and silently synchronized to the server's authoritative HP. Faint events also carry the authoritative final HP so the HUD reaches zero and finishes its drain before the sprite begins disappearing.
- Battle identity chips show a blue male or pink female symbol beside gendered Pokemon names, with genderless Pokemon left unmarked.
- AI logic for PvE opponents. On voluntary player switches, PvE trainer decisions are based on the Pokemon that was active before the switch, then the selected attack resolves into the switched-in Pokemon.
- PVE enemy AI uses a unified action scorer for stance attacks, special moves, configured trainer items, trainer switching, and forced trainer replacements after a KO. At battle start each enemy selects up to four AI move IDs from compatible authored moves plus any explicit `enemyTeam[].aiMoves` manual exceptions, then spends that enemy Pokemon's move budget only when a selected move is actually used. Authored enemy moves roll their configured accuracy when executed; misses skip damage, move effects, on-hit held effects, successful move history, and follow-up locks. Enemy-authored moves support the same battle conditions, effective-Speed contests, stance-disable effects, delayed damage queues, charged turns, recharge turns, continuous move locks, and self-pivot switches as player-authored moves; player-side battle rewards remain player-only. Normal stance attacks roll through the original weighted stance formula instead of countering the player's submitted stance, and move selection no longer scores against that hidden current-turn stance; final stance multipliers still resolve after both sides have chosen actions. Basic player and enemy stance attacks are normalized as no-cost internal 50 BP battle actions, while authored moves carry a move id and consume the move budget. Move decisions score damage, accuracy, type/weather effectiveness, active ability immunities and absorptions, healing, buffs/debuffs, status effects, secondary statuses, self-damage, interrupt potential, and move resource cost; defender-ability-bypass abilities remain an exception to those immunity checks. Move-level `aiUse` rules are no longer required. Enemy profiles add early/repeated move penalties and pace non-damaging effects against stance attacks, while damaging and valid called moves can still be selected when their own scoring and conditions justify them. Metronome remains above the generic stance fallback when it has a valid called move, so enemy Metronome usage resolves and is named instead of appearing as `Attack`. Secondary statuses are not refreshed unless missing or expiring, Protect-style damage-reduction moves are held for meaningful incoming damage and cannot repeat consecutively, enemy-targeted sleep application is not intentionally selected while the target is already asleep, and Mimic/Copycat-style calls fail AI selection until there is a valid authored opponent move to copy. Trainer switch and forced KO-replacement scoring evaluates basic player type options plus snapshotted player moves, and makes switching into an exposed weakness substantially less attractive. Trainer items are scored from their battle effects, powers remain player-only for enemy AI, and wild AI does not voluntarily switch. Basic stance attack data and stance multipliers remain unchanged.
- The PvP turn resolver accepts a persistence-disabled spectator mode and an injectable random source. Battle Bets uses that path for odds simulations and each live wager turn, preserving combat, status, switch, ability, held-item, and faint mechanics while skipping normal friendship, reward, KO, and held-item persistence writes. Spectator-only skipped actions carry trainer-item log messages through the same turn history. The Battle Bets client feeds each complete returned `BattleState` into `useBattleManager`, so its HUD, sprites, HP deltas, attacks, faints, replacements, and log use the normal battle presentation rather than a reduced replay renderer. Its terminal wager settlement separately records one mini-game win or loss and credits Fun Tokens only when the backed player-side team wins.
- Each owned Pokémon stores battle counters independently of its Pokédex entry: damaging super effective hits landed, stance victories, stance losses, battle KOs, times knocked out, and move actions used. Counters are staged by the battle engine and committed with the terminal PvE, PvP, or doubles outcome transaction; wild projections and Chronicle Pokémon are excluded.
- Mobile/PWA recovery: the client snaps to the latest confirmed battle state if visual animation timers stall, and re-fetches battle state if a server action stays in the resolving state too long.
